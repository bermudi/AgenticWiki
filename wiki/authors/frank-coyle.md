# Frank Coyle

> Educator at UC Berkeley with 30+ years in computer science. Background in neuroscience. Advocates for neuro-symbolic AI — combining LLMs with formal ontologies to create guardrails around agent loops. Speaker at AI Engineer events.

## Background

Coyle describes himself as an educator and teacher at Berkeley with 30–35 years of experience in computer science. His early career was in neuroscience, and he notes that Agent AI is bringing cognitive science back into relevance. He was involved in expert systems in the 1980s and witnessed both the rise and the AI winter that followed.

His educational philosophy draws from Sister Corita Kent (popularized by John Cage): "Nothing is a mistake. There is no win. There's no fail. There's only make." He advocates for hands-on learning — writing by hand, drawing, engaging sensory systems rather than just typing.

## Key Arguments

Coyle's central argument, delivered at an AI Engineer event (2026), is that agentic systems need ontologies — formal knowledge representations that provide guardrails around LLM agent loops. His key contributions:

1. **The neuro-symbolic convergence**: LLMs (probabilistic) meet ontologies (formal) to create a hybrid that retains LLM creativity while adding formal constraints.
2. **Bohm-Jacopini for agents**: Agent loops achieve Turing completeness through sequence, conditionals, and iteration — the same three constructs identified by Bohm and Jacopini (1966). This explains both the power and the danger of loops.
3. **Two-layer validation**: Pydantic for input type checking ("at the door"), ontologies for semantic domain validation ("at the ledger").
4. **Reclaiming expert systems**: The symbolic AI tradition — knowledge graphs, rule-based systems, ontologies — is not obsolete but is the missing piece for reliable agents.
